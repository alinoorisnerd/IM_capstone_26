## About Capstone Details

The Plot ...

The year is 2026! The world has progressed scientifically (still no flying cars). Digital information is no longer confined to screens; it now lives, eats, and breathes with us! It's everywhere! In fact, it has replaced laptops inside the classrooms. The story takes place at New York University Abu Dhabi. Students are going about their regular everyday chores, but calamity strikes one of the students. That student is YOU! The AI assistant embedded inside your glasses gets compromised via a Trojan file you downloaded from the internet. The system is hacked now! All of your assignments and data saved on the glasses will be deleted unless the demands of the AI are met. It's a race against time. You must:

  • - Read the demands of the system carefully as they are displayed on the screen and played through the speaker.
  • - After deciphering the hint, you must look for the object. Scan the objects by placing them in front of your glasses for at least 2 seconds.
  • - Make sure to find the items before the timer runs out!

A.R made you do it , but can you win ? Best of luck soldier!

About R.U.M.I ...

R.U.M.I stands for Reinforced User Media Interaction. At its core, the capstone is an Augmented Reality-based game designed to allow for maximum realism. Inspired by Myron Krueger's philosophy on minimal friction between the real world and the digital domain, I wanted to fuse the digital aspects of gameplay into the real world. Something lightweight that sits right in our field of vision was the best direction, so I settled on a lightweight attachment that can go with any glasses, allowing it to be used by people with different visual needs. The glasses interact with the user on three levels: vision, hearing, and intelligence.

Open Source Design ...

Initially, while working on this capstone, I was frequently asked what sets these glasses apart from existing technology. Currently, no consumer glasses facilitate custom AR-based gameplay; for example, Meta Ray-Ban and Even Realities’ G1 do not allow users to run their own applications. Therefore, it was imperative to build a solution from scratch. Developed over two semesters, these glasses were built upon the following key pillars:

  • - By projecting the display into the user’s eye at an infinite focus point, the image remains crystal clear—even when the distance from the lens to the eye is less than a millimeter. This was made possible through mathematically precise optical calculations.
  • - The custom circuitry and microcontrollers, embedded perfectly within the attachment's frame, power the entire project. This system facilitates real-time object detection and controls peripherals including the camera, a 1-watt speaker, and the heads-up display.
  • - These glasses are made by and for hackers and hobbyists. The code is hosted on GitHub and the CAD files are available on Onshape. Since mainstream AR glasses restrict custom development, R.U.M.I. provides the community with the opportunity to contribute and develop their own versions of the hardware..
